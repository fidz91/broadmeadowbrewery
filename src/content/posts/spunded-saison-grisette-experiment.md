Every farmhouse brewer knows the legend of the **Dupont Saison strain (`Wyeast 3724 / WLP565`)**: it produces unmatched citrus-pepper complexity, yet notoriously stalls around `1.030 SG` if it experiences back-pressure from a standard airlock or temperature drop.

In **Experiment #012**, we wanted to see what happens when we combine two variables on our **Broadmeadow Table Grisette** (`64.5% Belgian Pilsner`, `18.0% Malted Spelt`, `14.5% Flaked Wheat`, `3.0% Acidulated`):
1. Co-pitching `60% Wyeast 3724` with `40% Wyeast 3726 (Blaugies)` to guarantee full attenuation down to `1.004 FG`.
2. Comparing **Open Foil Primary + Late Spund (`#048A`)** against **12 PSI Pressure from Day 1 (`#048B`)**.

## What We Found

Fermenting under `12 PSI` from initial pitch (`#048B`) noticeably suppressed isoamyl acetate and 4-vinyl guaiacol production—resulting in a clean, slightly neutral blonde ale rather than an expressive Hainaut table beer.

By contrast, **Batch #048A**—fermented completely open under a crimped piece of sanitized aluminum foil for the first 72 hours (`1.039 -> 1.009 SG`) and then sealed with a spunding valve set to `18 PSI` at `21.5°C`—delivered the best of both worlds: explosive white pepper, gooseberry, and lemon zest on the nose, paired with champagne-fine natural carbonation (`2.9 volumes CO₂`).
