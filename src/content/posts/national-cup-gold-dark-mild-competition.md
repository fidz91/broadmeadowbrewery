Entering low-ABV British cask styles into bottled homebrew competitions is notoriously tricky. A Dark Mild or Ordinary Bitter that tastes sublime pulled through a sparkler at `10°C` in your own taproom can easily lose its delicate aromatics or suffer from over-carbonation when shipped in a `500 mL` crown-capped bottle.

This September, we entered **Batch #042 of 'Bramble & Bark' Dark Mild** into the **National Amateur Brewers Cup 2026** in Chester—and walked away with **Gold (`43.5 / 50`)** out of 28 entries in the British Brown & Mild table!

## How We Packaged a Cask-Conditioned Style for Bottled Judging

Judges evaluate Dark Mild (`BJCP 13A`) expecting low-to-moderate carbonation (`1.5 to 2.0 volumes CO₂`) and zero dissolved oxygen pickup:

1. **Carbonation Adjustment**: Two days before bottling from our keezer, we raised the keg pressure slightly from `1.6 vol` to **`1.95 volumes CO₂`** so that the `0.15 vol` lost during counter-pressure filling and pouring at the judging table would leave the glass right in the sweet spot (`1.8 volumes`).
2. **Closed-Loop Counter-Pressure Filling**: Every bottle was sanitized with peracetic acid, purged three times to `12 PSI` with pure $\text{CO}_2$, filled slowly down chilled glass walls, and capped on foam.

## Turning Scoresheet Notes into Iteration v4

Even on a `43.5` Gold scoresheet, good judges leave breadcrumbs for improvement. One Master BJCP judge wrote:

> "World-class texture and dark fruit depth. As the sample warms past 12°C, the toasted cocoa note from the roast malt edges slightly ahead of the plum/raisin crystal character. Dropping the dark roast malt by half a percent could make this a Best-of-Show contender."

We took that advice straight into **Batch #049 (v4)**, reducing **Thomas Fawcett Pale Chocolate** from `4.0%` down to `3.5%` and shifting `0.5%` back into our Crisp Maris Otter base.
